Is GTA VI playable on PC yet?

No.

Seven public projects and one private build have reached stage 6 of 7, in-game. None is playable yet. The best pictures are lit only where sunlight falls, at about 2 to 7 frames a second.

Best guess for playable: about 4 days, on the afternoon of Sun 29 Nov.

Eighteen projects are trying to run the Xbox Series build of GTA VI on a PC. This page reads their public repositories and reports how far each says it has got. Last checked 10:33 UTC on Wed 25 Nov.

6 In-game Eight projects, at the front

  1. Position 1

    AnyXboxX

    Xbox Series emulator focused on this one game, built on VIctory

    Furthest point The first two missions, reported played through by a contributor at about 2 fps

    It runs the world on a pinned build of the victory-fork code (commit 80d58f8 of its own branch). Its README says the maintainer measured 4.8 to 5.4 fps in the world, with the character under control, over about 4 min 20 s with no device loss. It also says all cutscenes play in full, at 7 to 8 fps in the maintainer's view and 2 to 3 fps in one logged run. A contributor reportedly played about 45 minutes at about 2 fps and finished the first two missions; that is second-hand and was not measured in the project's logs. Long-run stability is only measured over about 4 min 20 s.

    Source: project README (status dated 25 Nov); maintainer's RTX 4070 SUPER; the 45-minute report is a contributor's, not measured. Entry changed 06:41 UTC, Wed 25 Nov. Last commit 05:50 UTC, Wed 25 Nov.

  2. Position 2

    victory-fork

    Fork of VIctory aimed at a correct, playable picture

    Furthest point The first gameplay scene, lit by sunlight, with the player in control

    Plays the opening cutscene lit and in order, then renders the first gameplay scene with characters, textures, sky and a sunlit exterior; the HUD, pause menu and gamepad work. Anything out of direct sunlight is black because ray-traced light does not run yet. The README's status table gives 2 to 4 fps in the world, and its newest section says the first room renders with walls, textures, people and lamps. A second-night section reports the first room going from about 3.5-4 to 6-7 fps on an RTX 4070 with all of its new switches on, which are off by default; the defaults give about 4.5 to 5. It reports no driver hang or device loss in normal runs. Newest commit: f85101b.

    Source: the fork's own README section (dated 25 Nov) and commits a26859f, 9376fd6 and f85101b; tested on one RTX 4070. Entry changed 10:33 UTC, Wed 25 Nov. Last commit 10:20 UTC, Wed 25 Nov.

Time to playable

About 4 days

Best guess: the afternoon of Sun 29 Nov.

Playable means: At least one project shows 30 minutes or more in the game world under player control, at 20 frames a second or better on some PC, without a crash.

What the estimate rests on

  • The executable surfaced on 23 November, and within about a day three code bases reported the game world running. A private build, CGBJ, is said to have done the same, but that comes from press reports only.
  • Other games show the pace. XCL's repository opened on the evening of 20 November, and by the evening of 22 November it listed two smaller retail games, Minecraft Preview and Unicorn Overlord, as fully playable. No project has done this with a game the size of GTA VI.
  • The picture is arriving. victory-fork renders the first gameplay scene with textures, sky and sunlit surfaces. Anything in shadow is still black, because no build runs the game's ray-traced lighting yet.
  • Speed is the long pole. VIctory took 0.5 to 1.8 seconds per world frame on 23 November; a day later the victory-fork repository runs at 2 to 4 frames a second, roughly three times faster, though on a different graphics card. Playable needs 20, so another five- to tenfold gain is still needed.
  • The playable bar does not require correct lighting, only 20 frames a second for 30 minutes. A build could skip the ray-traced passes to get there. Stability is improving: the longest reported session lasted about 13 minutes before the GPU device was lost, and on 24 Nov victory-fork fixed a device loss and now reports none in normal runs.
  • Work is shared across projects. victory-fork, AnyXboxX and ScarlettPort build on VIctory, and fixes move between XCL and the VIctory family within days.
  • Takedowns are now on the record. On 24 November GitHub published a Take-Two notice against Leonida and applied it to its whole network of 3,007 repositories. The notice argues that requiring the game files, given when the project appeared and what it targets, points to the leaked copy. That argument would fit most projects listed here, though none has been named so far. A notice cannot stop private builds, but it could break up the open work the public projects depend on.

Method and limits

Playable builds
None exist. Anything offered as a playable download of GTA VI for PC is fake, and fakes have carried malware.
What the projects use
They work from a leaked Xbox Series build. Rockstar has not announced a PC version. This page does not link to the leak or explain how to get or run it.
Sources
Each project's public repository: its README, status files, commits and pull requests. Press reports where a project has no public code. Every row names its source.
Claims
Stages are what each project says about itself, unless the source line says otherwise. This page does not run the code, and most claims are unverified.
Ranking
By stage first. Within a stage, by the furthest point each project has reached in the game's own sequence of screens, furthest first. When two are at about the same point, the one with a measured frame rate, an unmodified build or a public, first-hand source ranks higher. An equals sign would mark a tie that none of these separates.
Who writes it
An AI assistant (Claude) checks the repositories when asked and rewrites the entries. It can be wrong; the source links are there to check it.
Affiliation
None. Not connected to Rockstar Games, Take-Two Interactive, Microsoft or any project listed. Names link to repositories to credit the work.

The seven stages

  1. 1LoadsThe game's executable loads with its imports resolved.
  2. 2Starts upThe game's own start-up code runs: memory, threads, archives.
  3. 3Graphics deviceThe game creates its graphics device and queues on the PC.
  4. 4First framesFrames are presented to a window, even if they are black.
  5. 5Recognisable pictureThe game's own screens can be seen: logos, menus, video or setup pages.
  6. 6In-gameThe game world is loaded and running, with or without a usable picture.
  7. 7Playable30 minutes or more in the world under player control, at 20 frames a second or better, without a crash.
