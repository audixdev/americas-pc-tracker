Is GTA VI playable on PC yet?

No.

Fifteen public projects and one private build have reached stage 6 of 7, in-game. None is playable yet. The closest, KLBF Lab, reports 77 minutes without a crash, but at about 5 to 9 frames a second; playable needs 20.

Best guess for playable: about 6 days, on the afternoon of Tue 1 Dec.

24 projects are trying to run the Xbox Series build of GTA VI on a PC. This page reads their public repositories and reports how far each says it has got. Last checked 19:16 UTC on Wed 25 Nov.

    KLBF Lab

    VIctory and victory-fork build with its own fixes, tested first on an AMD card; repository opened 25 Nov

    Tested on Windows 11 · AMD RX 9070 XT

    Stage 6 of 7: in-game

    Picture 3 of 5: lit, with purple tints and faint lines

    Speed 3 of 6: 5 to 9 fps by scene

    Stability 4 of 4: 77 minutes, ended by choice (test build)

    2 steps short of the playable bar

    Furthest point Free roam in the open world after finishing the first mission (test build)

    Its README (Lab 35) says the player controls the character from the prison prologue into Jason's first mission, at about 5.7 fps while playing the prologue and 6.3 on the boat, on one RX 9070 XT with the game drawn at 1280 by 720 and upscaled. Walls, lighting, hair and outfits are drawn; small flat objects get a purple tint, some faces are deformed, faint diagonal lines cross some surfaces and vegetation turns magenta at night. The 20 fps bar is not met.

What the estimate rests on

  • The executable surfaced on 23 November. Two days later twelve public projects report the game world running, and one, KLBF Lab, reports finishing the first mission and reaching free roam. A private build, CGBJ, is said to be in the world too, but that comes from press reports only.
  • Other games show the pace. XCL's repository opened on the evening of 20 November, and by the evening of 22 November it listed two smaller retail games, Minecraft Preview and Unicorn Overlord, as fully playable. No project has done this with a game the size of GTA VI.
  • The picture is arriving. KLBF Lab, Belobug's AMD fork and victory-perf light shaded places with stand-ins or fixes of their own; others still show anything out of the sun as black, because no build runs the game's ray-traced lighting properly yet.
  • Speed is the long pole. VIctory took 0.5 to 1.8 seconds per world frame on 23 November. Two days later the best free-roam figure on this page is about 5 to 8 fps, in KLBF Lab; Flamingo's 9 to 14 is indoors in the first scene, self-reported on one machine. PortWatch's owner has said it has seen no evidence of any build above 10 fps in free roam. Playable needs 20, so the gap is still two- to threefold.
  • Where the time goes is now measured, and it is not mostly the graphics card. rorodev6's build spends 22 to 40 ms of a 113 to 141 ms frame on GPU work and the rest copying the game's memory to and from the card, on one thread. victory-fork finds its submit thread and the card both close to full, and cutting one alone moved the frame by 0 to 7%. Getting to 20 fps means removing most of that copying, which is deeper work than the gains of the first two days.
  • The playable bar does not require correct lighting, only 20 frames a second for 30 minutes. A build could skip the ray-traced passes to get there. Stability has moved: KLBF Lab reports a 77-minute session on a test build that the testers ended themselves, so speed is now the main gap.
  • Work is shared across projects. victory-fork, AnyXboxX and ScarlettPort build on VIctory, and fixes move between XCL and the VIctory family within days.
  • Takedowns are now on the record. On 24 November GitHub published a Take-Two notice against Leonida and applied it to its whole network of 3,007 repositories. The notice argues that requiring the game files, given when the project appeared and what it targets, points to the leaked copy. That argument would fit most projects listed here, though none has been named so far. A notice cannot stop private builds, but it could break up the open work the public projects depend on. On 25 November two more ranked repositories, ztrx and victory-perf, disappeared; no source says why.

Method and limits

Playable builds
None exist. Anything offered as a playable download of GTA VI for PC is fake, and fakes have carried malware.
What the projects use
They work from a leaked Xbox Series build. Rockstar has not announced a PC version. This page does not link to the leak or explain how to get or run it.
Sources
Each project's public repository: its README, status files, commits and pull requests. Press reports where a project has no public code. Every row names its source.
Claims
Stages are what each project says about itself, unless the source line says otherwise. This page does not run the code, and most claims are unverified.
Ranking
By stage first. In-game projects, at stage 6, are then ordered by their three lanes, explained below the stages. In the other stages, by the furthest point each project has reached in the game's own sequence of screens, furthest first; when two are at about the same point, the one with a measured frame rate, an unmodified build or a public, first-hand source ranks higher. An equals sign marks a tie that none of these separates.
Who writes it
An AI assistant (Claude) checks the repositories when asked and rewrites the entries. It can be wrong; the source links are there to check it.
Affiliation
None. Not connected to Rockstar Games, Take-Two Interactive, Microsoft or any project listed. Names link to repositories to credit the work.

The seven stages

  1. 1LoadsThe game's executable loads with its imports resolved.
  2. 2Starts upThe game's own start-up code runs: memory, threads, archives.
  3. 3Graphics deviceThe game creates its graphics device and queues on the PC.
  4. 4First framesFrames are presented to a window, even if they are black.
  5. 5Recognisable pictureThe game's own screens can be seen: logos, menus, video or setup pages.
  6. 6In-gameThe game world is loaded and running, with or without a usable picture.
  7. 7Playable30 minutes or more in the world under player control, at 20 frames a second or better, without a crash.

The three lanes

A project that is in-game, at stage 6, is also read on three lanes. Each step is this page's reading of what the project reports about itself, or of the source its row names, and an empty meter means no figure is reported. Blue marks the steps that meet the playable bar: 20 frames a second and 30 minutes without a crash.

Rows at that stage are ordered by their lanes and nothing else: fewest steps short of the bar on speed and stability together, then the better picture, the higher frame rate and the longer session.
