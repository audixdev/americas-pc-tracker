Is GTA VI playable on PC yet?

No.

Five public projects and one private build have reached stage 6 of 7, in-game. None is playable yet. The best picture is lit only where sunlight falls, at 2 to 4 frames a second.

Best guess for playable: about 4½ days, on the afternoon of Sun 29 Nov.

Nineteen projects are trying to run the Xbox Series build of GTA VI on a PC. This page reads their public repositories and reports how far each says it has got. Last checked 20:22 UTC on Tue 24 Nov.

6 In-game Six projects, at the front

  1. Position 1

    victory-gta6

    Fork of VIctory aimed at a correct, playable picture

    Furthest point The first gameplay scene, lit by sunlight, with the player in control

    Plays the opening cutscene lit and in order, then renders the first gameplay scene with characters, textures, sky and a sunlit exterior; the HUD, pause menu and gamepad work. Anything out of direct sunlight is black because ray-traced light does not run yet. The world runs at 2 to 4 frames a second, or 240 to 350 ms a frame with opt-in switches. A device loss added by a wave64 update was fixed on 24 Nov, and the README now reports no hang or device loss in normal runs.

    Source: the fork's own README section (dated 25 Nov) and commit e9765a7, tested on one RTX 4070. Entry changed 20:04 UTC, Tue 24 Nov. Last commit 20:13 UTC, Tue 24 Nov.

    Xbox Series emulator focused on this one game, built on VIctory

    Furthest point About 13 minutes in the starting area with a picture, at about 4 fps

  3. Position 3

    ScarlettPort

    Packages VIctory and XCL with its own fixes and launcher; started 24 Nov

    Furthest point The movement tutorial prompt; the 3D picture behind it is black

The final picture is black because some lighting shaders do not translate yet; a debug view shows the world's geometry. A second path on XCL runs the menus and loading screens at 30 fps.

    Source: project README and commit 87116d5 (24 Nov), one RTX 3060; this page has not checked its prebuilt launcher. Entry changed 20:04 UTC, Tue 24 Nov. Last commit 19:43 UTC, Tue 24 Nov.

  4. Position 4

    VIctory

    Runs the game's Xbox code natively on Windows

    Furthest point The first mission's title card; the world behind it stays dark

    The upstream code base that victory-gta6 and AnyXboxX build on. Its README boots the game, runs the menus at 30 fps and loads into the world in about 50 seconds, where the first mission's title card renders but the scene after it stays dark, at 0.5 to 1.8 seconds a frame. Entering the world can hang a real graphics card.

    Source: project README (status dated 23 Nov), tested on one RX 6700 XT. Entry changed 19:30 UTC, Tue 24 Nov. Last commit 09:53 UTC, Tue 24 Nov.

  5. Position 5

    Palmwave

    Xbox Series to Windows layer with its own shader path

    Furthest point The first mission, with part of its geometry drawn and the picture one flat colour (modified run)

    The first mission starts and part of the world's geometry is drawn, such as poles, power lines and fences, but about 91% of the depth buffer is empty and the picture is one flat colour because nothing is lit yet. A world frame takes about 10 seconds. The runs use an unmerged branch and ten byte patches to the game, which switch off its hang detector and several heavy render passes.

    Source: project README; a modified run on an unmerged branch, RTX 5080. Entry changed 19:30 UTC, Tue 24 Nov. Last commit 16:22 UTC, Tue 24 Nov.

    Solo clean-room runtime, started from Leonida's work

    Furthest point A window shows presented frames, all black

Time to playable

About 4½ days

Best guess: the afternoon of Sun 29 Nov.

Playable means: At least one project shows 30 minutes or more in the game world under player control, at 20 frames a second or better on some PC, without a crash.

What the estimate rests on

  • The executable surfaced on 23 November, and within about a day three code bases reported the game world running. A private build, CGBJ, is said to have done the same, but that comes from press reports only.
  • Other games show the pace. XCL's repository opened on the evening of 20 November, and by the evening of 22 November it listed two smaller retail games, Minecraft Preview and Unicorn Overlord, as fully playable. No project has done this with a game the size of GTA VI.
  • The picture is arriving. victory-gta6 renders the first gameplay scene with textures, sky and sunlit surfaces. Anything in shadow is still black, because no build runs the game's ray-traced lighting yet.
  • Speed is the long pole. VIctory took 0.5 to 1.8 seconds per world frame on 23 November; a day later the victory-gta6 fork runs at 2 to 4 frames a second, roughly three times faster, though on a different graphics card. Playable needs 20, so another five- to tenfold gain is still needed.
  • The playable bar does not require correct lighting, only 20 frames a second for 30 minutes. A build could skip the ray-traced passes to get there, as Palmwave already does with other heavy passes in its modified runs. Stability is improving: the longest reported session lasted about 13 minutes before the GPU device was lost, and on 24 Nov victory-gta6 fixed a device loss and now reports none in normal runs.
  • Work is shared across projects. victory-gta6, AnyXboxX and ScarlettPort build on VIctory, and fixes move between XCL, VIctory and Palmwave within days.
  • Takedowns are now on the record. On 24 November GitHub published a Take-Two notice against Leonida and applied it to its whole network of 3,007 repositories. The notice argues that requiring the game files, given when the project appeared and what it targets, points to the leaked copy. That argument would fit most projects listed here, though none has been named so far. A notice cannot stop private builds, but it could break up the open work the public projects depend on.

Method and limits

Playable builds
None exist. Anything offered as a playable download of GTA VI for PC is fake, and fakes have carried malware.
What the projects use
They work from a leaked Xbox Series build. Rockstar has not announced a PC version. This page does not link to the leak or explain how to get or run it.
Sources
Each project's public repository: its README, status files, commits and pull requests. Press reports where a project has no public code. Every row names its source.
Claims
Stages are what each project says about itself, unless the source line says otherwise. This page does not run the code, and most claims are unverified.
Ranking
By stage first. Within a stage, by the furthest point each project has reached in the game's own sequence of screens, furthest first. When two are at about the same point, the one with a measured frame rate, an unmodified build or a public, first-hand source ranks higher. An equals sign would mark a tie that none of these separates.
Who writes it
An AI assistant (Claude) checks the repositories when asked and rewrites the entries. It can be wrong; the source links are there to check it.
Affiliation
None. Not connected to Rockstar Games, Take-Two Interactive, Microsoft or any project listed. Names link to repositories to credit the work.

The seven stages

  1. 1LoadsThe game's executable loads with its imports resolved.
  2. 2Starts upThe game's own start-up code runs: memory, threads, archives.
  3. 3Graphics deviceThe game creates its graphics device and queues on the PC.
  4. 4First framesFrames are presented to a window, even if they are black.
  5. 5Recognisable pictureThe game's own screens can be seen: logos, menus, video or setup pages.
  6. 6In-gameThe game world is loaded and running, with or without a usable picture.
  7. 7Playable30 minutes or more in the world under player control, at 20 frames a second or better, without a crash.
